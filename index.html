Four characters work. Six is better — a four-character square is about 176 km across, which spreads sunrise over some eleven minutes.

Map

The globe turns — drag it to bring the far side round, and it returns to your grid on its own. Flat shows the whole terminator at once, which is what long-path grey line needs.

Detail

Country borders are drawn dashed where the source marks them disputed. The field grid is the first pair of a locator — 20° of longitude by 10° of latitude, lettered along the top and left.

      About Worldpane

      An always-on display for a radio shack: the day and night terminator, sun times with the grey-line countdown, space weather including solar wind, and the next amateur satellite pass. No installation, no account, no server.

      Where it came from

      Worldpane shares no code with HamClock. It exists because HamClock, by Elwood Downey (WB0OEW, SK January 2026), showed what a shack display could be. The original stops functioning in June 2026.

      Worldpane deliberately does less than any of the projects below — it is one operator’s instrument rather than a dashboard for everyone. If you want the full article, with DX cluster, satellites and dozens of panels, these carry it on:

      • OpenHamClock — hosted and open source, the heavyweight
      • HamPulse — Apple TV and iPad
      • HAMSignal — native iOS and iPadOS
      • Shane Burrell’s dashboard — browser-based, DX cluster and APRS
      • hamclock.me

      What it is built from

      • Natural Earth coastline, country boundaries and lakes — public domain
      • NOAA SWPC — solar flux, planetary K and A, GOES X-ray, solar wind
      • CelesTrak — amateur satellite element sets
      • SatNOGS DB by the Libre Space Foundation — satellite frequencies and modes, CC BY-SA 4.0
      • satellite-js by Shashwat Kandadai — MIT, the SGP4 propagator behind the pass countdown
      • astronomy-engine by Don Cross — MIT, its licence text ships inside the vendored file

      Your data

      Your grid square and callsign are kept on this device and never sent anywhere. There is no account, no server and no analytics. The only network requests Worldpane makes are for space weather from NOAA and satellite element sets from CelesTrak.
